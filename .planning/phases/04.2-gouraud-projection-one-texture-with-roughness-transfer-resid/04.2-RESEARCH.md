## Summary

Phase 4.2 replaces the rejected 04.1 fit-driven sharp-removal search with a by-construction residual collapse. The core mechanism is already sitting in the codebase: `CSRasterizeVertexColors` (NAMERDecomp.compute:111-113) evaluates the quantized Color32 Gouraud surface per-texel with first-covering-triangle-wins. If the cleaned base handed to `CSResidual` is exactly that rasterization, then dividend == divisor at every covered texel and the quotient is 1.0 by IEEE identity (x/x == 1.0 exactly for finite non-zero x) — whiteness stops being an approximation quality and becomes a computation identity. The offline replication of the full fit → quantize → rasterize → quotient chain on the real Neo asset confirms: deviation is exactly 0.0 everywhere the interpolated vc is at or above the 1e-3 VcFloor, with a measured exception class of 62,447 texels (1.70% of covered) where a vc channel falls below the floor and 11,001 texels with an exactly-zero channel (dark-mesh B channel). A one-line symmetric floor in CSResidual removes even that class at the cost of sub-LSB reconstruction error.

The blocking research question — UV-overlap prevalence on Neo — is answered empirically. The Neo split mesh stacks UVs deliberately (mean depth ≈ 5.0, total UV area 4.363 UV-units² over 3.67M covered texels): 77.0% of all texels are covered by ≥2 triangles (87.9% of covered texels), 10.6% single-covered, 12.4% uncovered. The on-disk 04.1 residual EXR's non-white texels (6.25% of the atlas) are 92% concentrated on overlap texels (conditional non-white rate 7.3% overlap vs 3.2% single, ~2.3x enrichment; the 1.4% uncovered non-white rate is rasterizer-convention dust). Critical reframe: under the projection design, overlaps no longer threaten the whiteness claim at all (dividend == divisor regardless of which triangle won); they matter only as (a) fit-fidelity degradation — overlapping triangles impose mutually inconsistent LSQ constraints that the unweighted vertex mean blends — and (b) fidelity of the optional residual-ON path when computed against the pre-projection source. In one-texture mode (residual unbound, `"white" {}` default) overlaps cannot affect rendering because albedo comes from per-triangle 3D vertex-color interpolation with no texture lookup.

The roughness transfer has measured headroom. Removed-detail Rec.601 luminance (source − projected, linear) on Neo: 66.9% of texels are dark-response (mean magnitude 0.061), 33.1% bright (mean 0.108), energy split roughly 53/47 — a bright-only transfer would discard two-thirds of texels. |luma| percentiles: p50 0.059, p90 0.173, p99 0.273, max 0.469; a p90 normalization (mirroring RobustSobelScale) at strength 1.0 dips p90 texels by 63/63 levels (full clip), while raw unnormalized magnitudes never clip a scalar-0.5 roughness (|luma| > 0.5 on 0.000% of texels). Chroma loss is bounded: luminance retains 55% of removed-signal energy on average (p10 37%); discarded chroma magnitude averages 0.071 linear.

**Primary recommendation:** Implement the projection by re-running the existing fit + rasterization against the D-05 cleaned base and writing the rasterized surface back as the cleaned base (float path, no 8-bit intermediate); floor the dividend symmetrically in CSResidual (`max(base, VcFloor)`); feed signed removed-luminance (normalized by its own p90, Rec.601, computed by a small new kernel) into the unchanged D-08 remap consume site with the taste slider; keep the residual checkbox semantics as "compute residual against the pre-projection source base"; delete the ladder, fit cache, frequency separation, and estimator branches wholesale; report overlap stats honestly in the UI without attempting repair.

### Pattern 1: Rasterizer-as-dividend (the projection)
**What:** The cleaned base passed downstream becomes the output of the *same* `CSRasterizeVertexColors` pass that the residual kernel uses to recompute vcInterp. Not a new projection kernel — a reordering of existing dispatches.
**When to use:** Always when decomposition is on (this is the phase's core).
**Why it works:** `CSResidual` recomputes vcInterp with the same kernel, same triangle order, same arithmetic → dividend == divisor bit-exact at every covered texel → quotient exactly 1.0 (IEEE x/x identity) wherever vc >= VcFloor. Verified offline end-to-end: deviation is exactly 0.0 on all non-floor texels [VERIFIED: measurement, /tmp/namer_projection_check.py on Neo].
**Constraints discovered:**
1. Keep the dividend in the float path. Routing through an 8-bit intermediate makes the quotient `round(v*255)/255 / v`, deviating up to ~0.5/m LSB-relative — for Neo's dark VCs (mean rgb ~30/23/6 out of 255) that is up to ~10% at vc ~ 5/255, far above "8-bit dust". [VERIFIED: analysis; conservative bound]
2. Preserve source base alpha: the rasterizer writes coverage into A (0 on uncovered); the projection write-back must keep the source base's alpha channel or the residual identity branch (`baseA`) and the opaque-guard (`kOpaqueAlphaThreshold = 0.999`) misbehave. [VERIFIED: codebase, NAMERDecomp.compute CSRasterizeVertexColors + CSResidual identity branch]
3. First-covering-triangle-wins makes the projection deterministic (index order) — good; but it also means the projection's per-texel content on overlap texels belongs to the earliest triangle, not the "best" one. Accept; document.

### Pattern 2: Symmetric VcFloor
**What:** In `CSResidual`, change `residual = base / max(vc, VcFloor)` to `residual = max(base, VcFloor) / max(vc, VcFloor)` (per-channel).
**When to use:** Recommended default. With base == vc (projection), this makes the quotient exactly 1.0 everywhere including the below-floor class, and runtime reconstruction stays exact (albedo = 1 x vc = vc). The current asymmetric floor produces residual = vc/floor < 1 below the floor and reconstruction vc^2/floor — a darkening distortion bounded at 2.5e-4 linear (~0.8/255 sRGB, sub-LSB). [VERIFIED: analysis + measurement of the 1.70% exception class]
**Caveat:** Changes legacy (non-projection) residual semantics slightly for dark dividends; tests that pin current dark-texel residual values must be checked.

### Pattern 3: Luminance transfer through the D-08 site
**What:** A small kernel computes `luma = Rec601(sourceBase - projectedBase)` (signed), a reduce/normalize pass divides by p90(|luma|) mirroring `RobustSobelScale`, and the existing `CSRoughnessRemap` consumes it: `saturate(_ScalarRoughness - _Strength * luma_normalized)`. Signed feed-in is the recommended single polarity treatment: bright speckle (luma > 0) dips toward gloss (physical), dark occlusion (luma < 0) raises roughness toward matte (the stylization-physical direction) — one formula, one sign convention, both phenomena served.
**Measured basis (Neo):** dark-response texels are 66.9% of covered (mean mag 0.061), bright 33.1% (mean mag 0.108); a bright-only clamp would leave two-thirds of texels untransferred. |luma| p90 = 0.173; bright-only p90 = 0.228; dark-only p90 = 0.125. [VERIFIED: measurement; offline fit vs PNG round-trip base — absolute values carry ~10-20% noise from the 8-bit sRGB round-trip in dark channels, distribution shape robust]
**Headroom (6-bit):** with p90 normalization at strength 1.0, p90 texels dip 63/63 levels (full clip; `saturate` already guards); at strength 0.3, p50 -> ~6 levels, p90 -> ~19 levels. Raw (unnormalized) magnitudes never exceed 0.469, so raw-at-strength-1 never clips a scalar-0.5 roughness. Recommend p90 normalization (D-08 precedent, asset-independent slider feel) + slider default low (~0.2-0.3). [VERIFIED: measurement]

### Pattern 4: Residual checkbox semantics (recommended)
**What:** Checkbox OFF (default): no residual EXR, one-texture bind — unchanged 04.1 outcome. Checkbox ON: compute `CSResidual` with dividend := pre-projection *source* cleaned base (not the projection) — a meaningful detail-preserving EXR (this is essentially what the current on-disk EXR is: 6.25% non-white, overlap-correlated). Computing it against the projection instead would produce a pure-white dead file.
**When to use:** Whenever the user wants the removed detail archived for later re-application.
**Bound:** overlap texels in this file carry first-wins vcInterp from the wrong triangle — the same 7.3%-non-white signature the current EXR shows; single-covered regions carry the true removed detail. Document in UI copy, do not repair.

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid
- **New projection kernel:** do not write a "Gouraud evaluator" that differs in any way (triangle order, texel-center convention, quantization) from `CSRasterizeVertexColors` — any divergence reintroduces quotient dust. Reuse the dispatch.
- **8-bit intermediate for the projected base:** breaks exact whiteness on dark VCs (see Pattern 1 constraint 1).
- **Threshold tuning:** any new epsilon/threshold that gates the residual re-creates the 04.1 honest-threshold gamble the phase exists to delete. The gate becomes the checkbox.
- **Overlap repair:** per-island re-rasterization or highest-area-wins would change the fitted-VC/rasterization contract for a fidelity gain only the optional residual path can observe. Repair is out of proportion; report instead.
- **CPU write-back loops:** the projection write-back is per-texel GPU work (CLAUDE.md performance rule).

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: The VcFloor exception class (measured)
**What goes wrong:** 1.70% of covered texels (62,447 on Neo) have an interpolated vc channel below `VcFloor = 1e-3` (11,001 exactly zero — Neo's B channel mean is 5.9/255 and 25% of fitted vertices quantize B to 0). There the quotient is vc/1e-3, deviating from white by up to ~1.0, even in the by-construction path.
**Why it happens:** Color32 quantization drives whole vertex channels to 0 on dark meshes; the floor is below one 8-bit LSB (1/255 = 0.0039), so only exact-zero/near-zero interpolations fall under it.
**How to avoid:** Symmetric floor (Pattern 2). If not adopted: the D-13 gate still passes (fit-only compare is 0 vs 0), reconstruction error is sub-LSB, but the residual file is visibly non-white in dark patches — document.
**Warning signs:** UAT on a dark asset sees black spots in the residual EXR's dark-VC regions.

### Pitfall 2: Alpha channel of the projected base
**What goes wrong:** Reusing the rasterization output as the cleaned base carries coverage-in-alpha (0 on uncovered texels); downstream `CSResidual` identity branch, the opaque guard, and PNG encoding all consume base alpha.
**How to avoid:** Write back projected RGB but source alpha from the pre-projection base (blend in the write-back dispatch or a copy pass).
**Warning signs:** Residual EXR alpha holes; D-13 opaque guard flips behavior.

### Pitfall 3: ChooseResolution criterion collapse (VCOL-05)
**What goes wrong:** The D-16 adaptive ladder selects resolution by fit-only MaxError, which is identically 0 under the projection → the ladder degenerates to the smallest rung ({2048..128}) for every asset.
**How to avoid:** Redefine the criterion when the checkbox is ON (e.g., reconstruction-vs-source error per resolution — measurable with the existing `CSErrorHeatmap` in non-fit-only mode), or pin the default and rely on D-17 manual override. Planner decision; recommendation: keep the ladder only for the residual-ON path, keyed on source-reconstruction error.
**Warning signs:** Residual EXRs silently at 128px.

### Pitfall 4: sRGB round-trips amplify relative error in dark channels
**What goes wrong:** Any 8-bit sRGB intermediate (PNG save/load) introduces up to ~0.0015 linear absolute error; at Neo's linear values (~0.01-0.03) that is 5-30% relative — enough to swamp quotient whiteness measurements and, in-product, any comparison against written PNGs. My offline forced-keep quotient measured ~100% non-white purely from this artifact while the engine's own float-path EXR is 93.75% white.
**How to avoid:** Keep in-pipeline comparisons in the float RT path; never gate pipeline logic on re-read PNG values.
**Warning signs:** Tests that read back written PNGs and assert tight linear tolerances on dark content.

### Pitfall 5: Measurement methodology (documented for test authors)
The UV-overlap analysis went through three rasterizer iterations before converging; the pitfalls are reproducibility traps for any future editor-side or offline verification:
- An always-true inside mask (from a mis-scaled barycentric inverse) combined with out-of-y-range scanline spans clamped to the bounding box silently over-covers — it admitted full bbox rows on slack scanlines, inflating overlap to a bogus 98.11%/0.18%-single split and saturating the depth counter (the 250-spike alone implied more coverage mass than the mesh's total UV area — an internal-consistency check that caught it).
- Unity YAML `_typelessdata` in this project is HEX, not base64 (base64 decode "succeeds" into garbage floats — look for the `000080bf` −1.0 marker).
- The split mesh's stream1 record is `[uv.x f32][uv.y f32][color32]` (empirically; the m_Channels table reading was misleading).
- Uncompressed EXR: half-float scanlines, channel order A,B,G,R interleaved, offset table (2048×8 bytes) between header and data.
- Texel-center convention: `px + 0.5`; spans from exact edge intersections; ceil/floor with a +1e-9 guard.
Two independent geometric implementations agreed on covered-union within 0.02% (3,673,095 vs 3,673,847 texels) — that agreement, plus the analytic single-triangle check (335,381 vs 335,544), is the confidence basis for the reported numbers. [VERIFIED: measurement, /tmp/namer_overlap_v3.py predecessor + /tmp/namer_projection_check.py]

### Pitfall 6: Retiring code that tests still pin
**What goes wrong:** The 04.1-07 dip-coupling tests pin ladder-driven dip behavior; wholesale kernel deletion breaks compilation of test assemblies.
**How to avoid:** Flip tests in the same task wave as the deletion (CONTEXT locks this posture); grep `NamerRoughnessExtractionTests.cs` / `NamerRoughnessFitTests.cs` for references to retired symbols (`RequestFit`, `RunSharpRemoval`, estimator enum) before deleting.

### Pitfall 7: Release routing after the D-05 repoint
`NormalizedBaseColor` is repointed to the cleaned base with `NormalizedBaseColorOwnedByRoughnessPool` release routing (NamerComputePipeline.cs:196-205). The projection write-back must respect that ownership (write into the owned RT or allocate per the existing pattern) or the pool double-releases. `_currentBaseColorOut` reentrancy guard applies.

## Code Examples

### The identity that makes whiteness exact
```hlsl
// Source: Compute/NAMERDecomp.compute:111-141 (verbatim behavior, lines from prior in-session read)
// CSRasterizeVertexColors: first covering triangle wins; uncovered -> (0,0,0,0)
// CSResidual:
//   residual = base.rgb / max(vc.rgb, VcFloor)   -- VcFloor = 1e-3
//   uncovered -> identity (1,1,1, baseA)
```
